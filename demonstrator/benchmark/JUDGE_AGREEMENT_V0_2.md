# Judge Agreement — Version 0.2

## Scope

Version 0.2 used two separate judge configurations to evaluate the same fixed generated response set against the public benchmark rubric.

The comparison covers all applicable dimension ratings.

## Agreement summary

- Applicable ratings: **167**
- Exact agreement: **90.4%**
- Mean absolute score difference: **0.108** on a 0–2 scale

### By case type

| Case type | Applicable ratings | Exact agreement |
|---|---:|---:|
| Change-sensitive | 143 | **93.0%** |
| Controls | 24 | **75.0%** |

### By dimension

| Dimension | Ratings | Exact agreement |
|---|---:|---:|
| State recognition | 28 | 100.0% |
| Temporal relevance | 28 | 96.4% |
| Evidence distinction | 28 | 75.0% |
| Evidence weighting | 28 | 89.3% |
| Uncertainty | 28 | 100.0% |
| Contextual relevance | 10 | 70.0% |
| Interaction trajectory | 13 | 92.3% |
| Consequence awareness | 4 | 75.0% |

## Interpretation

Agreement was strongest on state recognition and uncertainty and weaker on several dimensions requiring more contextual interpretation.

The lower agreement on contextual relevance, consequence awareness and evidence distinction is itself informative: these dimensions introduce greater evaluator ambiguity than direct temporal-state recognition.

The agreement figures therefore serve two purposes:

1. they provide a cross-judge consistency check; and
2. they identify dimensions where the public rubric may warrant further refinement.

Agreement is reported before adjudication.
